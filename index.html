FORGE A WORKING-CLASS VOICE

Every strong movement births its own voice. Workers for a New Southwest has formed an Editorial Board to support that work. The goal is to produce clear, principled writing that helps educate, inspire, and organize our class. Our aim goes beyond slogans and centers strong writing that explains our conditions, sharpens our politics, and resonates with working people.

WHY WRITING MATTERS

Writing is not useless busywork. It is part of the class struggle. When we write, we make sense of what we are fighting, share lessons from on-the-ground organizing, and record the history of our people and our land. Writing gives workers tools to act strategically, with understanding. A disciplined writing culture makes sense of experience and builds the kind of knowledge we can act on.

WHAT THE EDITORIAL BOARD DOES

The Editorial Board delivers thoughtful, critical feedback to hone writers’ skills. It supports contributors by editing drafts for clarity and structure, while sharpening political acuity. The board prepares pieces for publication to a wider audience and also keeps the public voice of WNS consistent. All of this is collaborative work, not top-down control. Writers stay involved in the process, since the goal is to develop people, not just produce content.

HOW THE PROCESS WORKS

The process begins here by visiting our Writers’ Portal. Prospective writers can accept an assignment from our list of prompts or submit a pitch for an original piece. Fill out the simple form, and an editor will get in touch with onboarding details, including a timeline from the first draft to publication. Writers are expected to communicate accountably and agree to our standards of intellectual integrity (see “Our Standards”). Once a draft is submitted, the Editorial Board will provide critical feedback and suggest edits within seven days. The draft is returned with notes so the writer can improve their writing. Once the piece is ready, it is prepared for publication on our Substack. Urgent political statements can move faster when needed.

WHAT YOU CAN WRITE

Cadre, core, and members of the WNS community are all encouraged to write regularly. This includes public statements from the organization, articles for Substack or other platforms, and political analysis tied to current events. Members can also write about labor, land, and other working-class struggles, as well as the history of working-class resistance in the Southwest. Some pieces speak for the organization as a whole, while others allow room for analysis and debate; both forms of writing are important. Ideas for other creative pieces are welcome, pending approval by the board. Approval is based on political and/or cultural alignment with our organization. Click this link or the russet button at the top of this page to apply: Write for WNS.

PUBLIC STATEMENTS

Public Statements are official positions of Workers for a New Southwest. They respond to major events, express solidarity or opposition, and clarify our stance on key issues affecting working people. These statements are written or approved by cadre to ensure political clarity and unity. The Editorial Board helps refine them, but the political responsibility rests with leadership.

OUR STANDARDS

We aim for writing that is clear and direct, rooted in real conditions, and disciplined in tone. Our work must respect the struggles and cultures of the Southwest while staying politically sharp. Writing should help people study, organize, and take action. We do not accept pieces that are the product of plagiarism or artificial intelligence because our credibility as an organization depends on the integrity of our work.

JOIN THE EDITORIAL BOARD

In March 2026, WNS cadre established the first Editorial Board to carry this work forward. As our work evolves and expands, so too will our team. Participation is self-selected, meaning that WNS core members may step forward and volunteer. Each new member to the board completes a three-month trial period to ensure the role is a good fit and that the board as a whole stays active and reliable. This process helps maintain discipline while giving those interested the space to contribute their skills.

To join the Editorial Board, contact the Board Helm at wns.editorial.board@gmail.com.

PLUG IN

You can get involved right here at the Writers’ Portal. Fill out the form to pitch your idea or accept an assignment. You can also volunteer to join the board, work with others on collaborative writing projects, or help build a culture where writing is part of everyday organizing. You can help make writing a shared practice across the organization.

A movement that cannot explain itself cannot grow. Through steady writing and careful editing, we can clarify our politics, train our cadre, and speak directly to working people. This work helps shape working-class consciousness across New Mexico and strengthens our ability to organize and build a thriving political culture.
